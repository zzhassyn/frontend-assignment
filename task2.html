This page is engineered strictly using traditional float: left, float: right, and clear: both.

Understanding CSS Float & Clear Properties

Before modern CSS Flexbox and Grid were standardized, web developers relied entirely on the float property to create multi-column website layouts. Originally designed to let text wrap around images, floating an element takes it out of normal block flow and shifts it to the left or right edge of its parent.

When two adjacent block elements are floated (one left and one right), they sit side by side horizontally. This created the classic two-column and three-column website designs of the early web.

The Role of Clearfix (clear: both)

Because floated children are taken out of the standard layout flow, their parent container cannot measure their height. Without a clear mechanism, the parent collapses to zero height.

To prevent collapse, we place a divider with clear: both; immediately below the floated columns, forcing the parent to fully enclose them.
